# Levels: grade systems, plain words and how to pick one

Read this when the user's level is in words ("beginner", 初学), described by time ("one year of lessons"),
or given in a system other than ABRSM (Trinity, RCM, AMEB, a Chinese board). It is our summary, in our
own words, of public documents; every source is in [sources.md](sources.md) and ABRSM's, Trinity's and
the others' own documents are the authority. Compiled 2026-10-14.

## What is official and what is our convention

- **Official**: ABRSM and Trinity College London both run Initial plus Grades 1–8 and sit in the same UK
  qualification-framework bands: Initial = Entry 3, Grades 1–3 = Level 1, Grades 4–5 = Level 2, Grades
  6–8 = Level 3, with the same stated study hours per grade (40, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 220, 270, 320).
  Same band and same hours do not mean same difficulty: their technical requirements differ.
- **Not official**: any practical equivalence between ABRSM and RCM, AMEB or the Chinese boards. None of
  those bodies publishes one (RCM publishes a theory-only table). Everything below the line "working
  default" is a convention of this skill, chosen to land a little *easier* than the user's level, so the
  result is playable; it is said out loud in the reply and the user can ask for the next step up.
- The piano profile and most instrument profiles use ABRSM numbers because ABRSM publishes parameter
  tables per grade. Trinity Rock & Pop (guitar, drums, voice) has its own ladder; where this skill uses it
  the instrument file says so.

## The ladder this skill works on

Initial (written 0 in the script) and Grades 1–8, as in ABRSM and Trinity. Below Initial: the
five-finger or open-string version (see each instrument file). Above Grade 8: not covered; say so, give
the Grade 8 version and name what a diploma-level piece adds.

| Band | Grades | In the UK framework | What it means for an arranger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beginner | Initial, 1 | Entry 3, Level 1 | One position or string set, simple keys, quarters and halves, almost no accompaniment |
| Elementary | 2, 3 | Level 1 | A few more keys, dotted rhythm, hands together, first sixteenths |
| Intermediate | 4, 5 | Level 2 | Shifts and wider positions, chromatic notes, syncopation, chords |
| Upper intermediate and advanced | 6, 7, 8 | Level 3 | Triplets, odd meters, wide range; most limits stop being numbers |

## Plain words and learner descriptions → a default level

These are this skill's convention for when the user gives no grade. They vary widely with age, practice
time and instrument; the exam boards publish study hours but not "years". Use the default, say it in
one line, and offer one step up and one step down.

| The user says | Default level |
| --- | --- |
| complete beginner, 入门, 刚开始学, a few weeks | Initial |
| beginner, first year, 初学, about 6–12 months | Grade 1 |
| elementary, easy, "a year or two", 初级 (loosely) | Grade 2 |
| two to three years, "can play simple pieces well" | Grade 3 |
| intermediate, 中级 (loosely), three to four years | Grade 4–5 (pick 4, offer 5) |
| upper intermediate, "plays real repertoire" | Grade 6 |
| advanced, 高级 (loosely), "nearly Grade 8" | Grade 7–8 (pick 7, offer 8) |

初级 / 中级 / 高级 are not standard: ABRSM's own Chinese specification uses 初级 for Initial, Chinese
teaching articles use 初级/中级/高级 for different spans of method books, and two draft rules suggest
tiers of two to four grades each. State the boundary you used ("初级 as Grades 1–2") and let the user
correct it. For an instrument with an Initial-less syllabus (most winds, brass, guitar) start the ladder
at Grade 1.

## Other grade systems → a working default

Convert once, tell the user the number you used in one line ("RCM 4, which I'm treating as about
ABRSM Grade 3"), and keep it as the target. If the user names a Chinese board, use the table; if they
only say "国内五级", ask which body (see below) unless they are happy with the default.

| System | Levels | Working default for piano | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ABRSM 英皇 | Initial, 1–8 | as is | 「英皇N级」 means ABRSM Grade N, normally the piano practical exam unless 乐理 is said |
| Trinity 三一 | Initial, 1–8 | same number as ABRSM | Same UK framework band; technical work differs |
| RCM 加拿大皇家音乐学院 | Prep A, Prep B, 1–10 | Prep A or B → Initial; Level N → ABRSM Grade N−1 for N = 2…9 (level 1 → Grade 1); Level 10 → Grade 8 | No official practical equivalence; RCM Level 8 theory = ABRSM Grade 5 theory only |
| AMEB 澳大利亚 | Preliminary, 1–8 | Preliminary → Initial; Grade N → ABRSM Grade N | Structural match only (a 0–8 ladder); nothing official |
| 中央音乐学院海内外考级 | 1–9, then 演奏级 | Level N → ABRSM Grade N−1 (N ≥ 2); 1 → Initial | Sources show no numbered 10级 for piano |
| 中国音乐学院 | 1–10 (some sources 11) | same as 央音 | Counts are inconsistent across sources |
| 中国音乐家协会（音协） | 1–10 | same as 央音 | Described by teachers as relatively lenient |
| 上海音乐学院 / 上海音协 | 1–10, then 演奏级 | same as 央音 | The two Shanghai systems are different bodies; popular articles say 「上音」 for both |

Chinese grade numbers run 1–10 while ABRSM has eight grades, and Chinese teachers disagree: ABRSM 8 is
often put near a domestic 10 on repertoire alone, ABRSM 6 near 音协 4, and a piece graded 央音 5 can
appear at 音协 9. Treat these as noise: the default above is deliberately on the easy side. For piano,
one Shanghai 音协 notice ties its levels to method books (level 1 ends Beyer; 3–4 Czerny op.849; 5–6
op.299; 8 op.740; 9 Moszkowski; 10 Chopin etudes), which is the best published sense of the climb.

For instruments other than piano, boards number their grades separately and nothing is published across
them; use the instrument's own ABRSM profile and the same N−1 default for RCM and the Chinese boards, and
say the confidence is low. Chinese instruments (二胡, 古筝, 竹笛) have their own ladders, in their files.

## How to say it

- 「约英皇三级难度」 / "about Grade 3" in titles and replies: a description, not a claim. No exam board
  logos, no "official", "approved", "exam piece" or "certified". Do not copy syllabus repertoire or
  scale lists into the reply.
- 「英皇」 is the common Chinese short name for ABRSM. We found no public ABRSM statement on third parties
  using 「英皇一级」 as plain description (its legal pages were unreachable); the safe use is the plain
  description above. This is not legal advice.
- In the user's language and words: if they said 「一年左右」, answer 「大概英皇一级」 and move on.

## What to ask when the level is unclear

One question, three or four options, one marked as the default; options in the user's words and, where
useful, with a rough grade in brackets:

- 「她大概学了多久？」 刚开始几个月（约入门到一级，默认）／一两年（二、三级）／三年以上（四级以上）
- "How long has the player been learning?" a few months (Initial–1, default) / a year or two (2–3) /
  three years or more (4+)

If the user names a Chinese board without the body ("国内三级"), ask: 央音 / 国院 / 音协 / 上音 / 不确定
(默认当作约英皇二级).
