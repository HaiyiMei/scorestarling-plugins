# Cello: difficulty by level (ABRSM)

**Read this when** the target instrument is cello, at any level. Numbers come from ABRSM Bowed Strings syllabus from 2024; the checker reads them from [../profiles/cello.json](../profiles/cello.json): `python3 scripts/check_level.py arrangement.musicxml --instrument cello --grade N`. S1, S2 … in the tables point to the Sources section at the end; the master list with a confidence note for each is [../sources.md](../sources.md). Compiled 2026-10-14.

Scope: ABRSM Practical Grades, Initial Grade and Grades 1-8, syllabus "from 2024" (valid from 1 Jan 2024 "until further notice"; the syllabus itself says scales, sight-reading and aural requirements are unchanged from the 2020-2023 syllabus). Pitches are scientific pitch (middle C = C4; the lowest cello note is C2). "SR" = the sight-reading test. Notation in cells: maj = major, min = minor, oct = octaves, q = crotchet, 3# = three sharps in the key signature.

## Table by grade

Scope reminder: the keys, time signatures, rhythms, ranges, dynamics and SR columns come from the official sight-reading test specification (S1), which says its parameters are cumulative (once introduced they apply at all later grades). It is a specification for the unseen test, NOT a limit on what the three set pieces contain; ABRSM publishes no equivalent table for pieces. "Scale ceiling" = highest note reached by the grade's scale/arpeggio list, computed by me from S1 requirement lists using "lowest possible tonic" (C2) or the stated open-string start. The SR table never names the clef before Grade 7; because tenor clef is introduced at G7 and treble at G8, bass clef is the evident default (inference).

Other documented points (S1): SR gives 30 seconds to look through and try out any part; fingering printed on the test is only a guide; SR is worth 21 of 150 marks, scales 21, each piece 30, aural 18 (Pass 100). All scales are played from memory, from the lowest possible tonic, with even notes or "long tonic" at the candidate's choice. Initial-Grade-to-Grade-3 pieces may be duets (candidate plays the upper part) and some set pieces are unaccompanied; list A pieces are generally faster and technical, B lyrical, C varied in tradition.

## What changes between neighbouring grades

- Initial to 1: range from two string sets (G2-C3, D3-G3) to one continuous span G2-D4; 3/4, minims, four-quaver groups, f and p; no new SR keys; A natural minor and 2-oct C maj appear in scales; slurred scales (2 quavers to a bow) begin.
- 1 to 2: low end drops from G2 to C2 (C string joins); C maj and a min; dotted minims, longer rests; two-note slurs; mp and hairpins; SR length doubles to 8 bars. First scale ceiling above D4 (G maj 2 oct to G4).
- 2 to 3: SR gains its first flat keys and first 3-sharp key (A, F, B-flat maj; d, g min); dotted rhythms, semiquaver patterns, ties, staccato, pizzicato; chromatic scale appears.
- 3 to 4: the largest SR step: shifts between 1st and 4th positions, 6/8, anacrusis, chromatic notes, hooked bows, tenuto/accents, pauses, pp and ff; SR range up to G4; dominant 7ths.
- 4 to 5: SR 8-16 bars; shifts "as required" (range to A4); syncopation; arco/pizzicato changes; rit.; simple chords; 3-octave C maj; diminished 7ths.
- 5 to 6: 9/8, 5/8, 5/4 and triplets; a tempo; thumb-position scale; broken-sixths double stops; no SR range growth; 3-oct C and D scales.
- 6 to 7: TENOR CLEF in SR; 7/8 and 7/4; SR to B-flat4; 16-20 bars; all scales 3 oct (D-flat/C#, E-flat, E, F); keys to 4#/4b.
- 7 to 8: TREBLE CLEF in SR; 12/8; ornaments; left-hand pizzicato; acceleration; SR to D5; parallel sixths, broken thirds and octaves.

## Arranging implications

Official limits (from S1) are marked [O]; my inferences are marked [I].
- Key: choose a key from the cumulative SR key list for the target grade [O]. Initial-G1: G or D major only; G2: add C, a; G3: add A, F, B-flat, d, g; G4: E-flat, c; G5: e; G7: E, A-flat, b, f; G8: f#. Transpose to the nearest listed key. Minor keys: none in SR before G2, natural form only at G2, any form from G3 [O]. Note that cello G6 adds no keys at all.
- Range: keep every note inside the SR range [O]: Initial G2-C3 and D3-G3; G1 G2-D4; G2-G3 C2-D4; G4 C2-G4; G5-G6 C2-A4; G7 C2-B-flat4; G8 C2-D5. To go down a grade, drop or octave-displace the top phrases first. [I] Pieces can exceed the SR range somewhat; the scale ceilings (D4, D4, G4, G4, B-flat4, C5, D5, F5, B-flat5 for Initial-G8) are the official upper bound of what candidates drill, so treat them as a hard stop.
- Position work: 1st position only at Initial [O]; no shifts below G4 [O]; G4 limited to 1st and 4th positions [O]; shifts as required from G5 [O]. [I] Below G4 keep melodies inside D4 (4th finger on the A string) and avoid string crossings that need extensions; this matches the G1-G3 SR range, which stops at D4.
- Thumb position: appears only in technical work at G6 (D major) and G7 (D major and minor) [O]; not mentioned in the SR table [O]. [I] Do not write thumb-position passages below G6; at G6-G7 keep them to a short, scalar passage on the D string.
- Clefs: write in bass clef for Initial-G6 [I from O: tenor clef enters SR at G7, treble clef at G8]. Tenor clef only at G7+, treble clef only at G8 [O]; ABRSM states no pitch threshold for when to switch, so [I] use tenor only where bass clef would need many ledger lines. Avoid clef changes below G7.
- Rhythm: strip the feature introduced at the grade above [O]. Initial: crotchets, paired quavers, crotchet rests; G1: minims, four-quaver groups; G2: dotted minim; G3: dotted crotchet-quaver, dotted quaver-semiquaver, ties, simple semiquavers; G4: anacrusis; G5: simple syncopation; G6: triplets. Avoid 6/8 below G4; 9/8, 5/8, 5/4 below G6; 7/8, 7/4 below G7; 12/8 below G8.
- Bowing: Initial and G1 write every note detached [O]; G2 allow two-note slurs [O]; hooked bowing from G4 [O]. [I] Technical work uses 2 quavers to a bow from G1 and 3-note slurs for arpeggios from G3, so 3-note slurs are defensible at G3 and 4-note slurs at G4; do not slur longer than that below G6.
- Articulation: staccato and pizzicato from G3; accents and tenuto from G4; left-hand pizzicato only at G8 [O] (cello list differs from violin, where it arrives at G7).
- Dynamics: Initial mf only; G1 f, p; G2 mp and hairpins; G4 pp, ff [O].
- Accidentals: none foreign to the key until G3 (minor-key accidentals only); free chromatic notes from G4 [O].
- Chords and double stops: simple chords at the very end of an SR from G5 [O]; broken-step sixths G6+, parallel sixths and broken thirds/octaves only G8 [O]. [I] Do not write double stops in a melody arranged below G5; at G5-G6 limit to a final chord.
- Vibrato: ABRSM says pieces heavily reliant on vibrato tend not to appear before about Grade 5 [O]; do not make an Initial-G4 arrangement depend on vibrato.
- Length and tempo: SR lengths 4-8 bars at Initial-G3, up to 16-24 at G7-G8 [O]; set-piece length is not published. [I] Keep arrangements short at low grades. Official scale speeds (quaver=76 at Initial, then crotchet 44 at G1 rising to 100 at G8) are the only tempo data [O]; [I] use them as a rough ceiling for sustained running notes.
- Going down one grade, in order: (1) transpose to a lower-grade key, (2) pull notes inside the lower SR range, (3) remove the newest rhythm and articulation items listed above, (4) delete shifts and any thumb-position or high-register writing, (5) drop dynamic markings above that grade's set.

## Confidence and gaps

- Keys: SR new-key lists verbatim from S1 (HIGH); the cumulative set is my assembly from ABRSM's statement that parameters are cumulative. Technical-work keys verbatim (HIGH). Keys used in set pieces: unknown.
- Time signatures: SR list verbatim (HIGH). Initial row shows two lines (4 bars with 4/4, 6 bars with 2/4); that pairing is how I read the rendered table (MEDIUM). Time signatures in set pieces: unknown.
- Note values and rhythms: SR verbatim, but several symbols (beamed quaver groups, the Grade 2 rest, dotted shapes at Grade 3) were read from images; my verbal renderings are MEDIUM-HIGH. Rhythm in set pieces: unknown.
- Range: SR ranges verbatim from S1 in Helmholtz notation; conversion to scientific pitch is mine (HIGH; capital C = C2 matches the printed stave). Scale ceilings are computed by me from official requirement lists (HIGH arithmetic; open-string starts used where stated). Typical range of set pieces: UNKNOWN; ABRSM does not publish it.
- Positions: official only for SR (1st position at Initial; 1st/4th shifts at G4; "as required" at G5) and for thumb-position scales (G6, G7). The remarks about the 4th finger reaching D4 and about shifts implied by G2-G3 scales are derived from note spans and standard cello geometry, not from ABRSM (MEDIUM). Positions in set pieces, and any use of half position or extensions: unknown.
- Clefs: tenor (G7) and treble (G8) verbatim; the assumption that G1-G6 SR is bass clef is inferred (HIGH likelihood). Clefs used in set pieces: unknown, may precede the SR introduction.
- Bowing/articulation: scale bowing and slur counts verbatim (HIGH); SR articulation verbatim (HIGH); piece bowing inferred.
- Dynamics: SR verbatim (HIGH); piece dynamics unknown.
- SR length: verbatim (HIGH); "c." is approximate in the original. No tempo is specified for SR.
- Scale speeds: verbatim from S1 p. 20 (HIGH); ABRSM calls them a general guide. Initial speed is a quaver value, later ones crotchet (as printed).
- Secondary sources add nothing quantitative; the profile rests on S1 (cross-checked against S2). The biggest gap is piece-level characteristics (range, positions, clefs, keys, rhythm in set pieces), which no free public source supplies. Fill it by sampling the published exam-piece books per grade if available.
